# Be a Little Braver

Create a premium cinematic beer commercial titled **Be a Little Braver**, approximately 53 seconds long, in 16:9 landscape format at 24 fps. Build a continuous story across the scenes below. Preserve the same protagonist, product design, visual language, and narrator throughout.

The emotional progression is quiet everyday independence, a wish to connect naturally, recognition that he can remain himself, and a small moment of connection. Keep the performance subtle and believable.

## Main Character

A Chinese male office worker, age 25–27, with a lean but healthy build, natural short black hair, clean-shaven face, straight dark eyebrows, and fresh, ordinary facial features. He looks approachable and authentic rather than like a celebrity or fashion model.

He is quiet, reserved, slow to warm up, and emotionally grounded. He would like to meet people but does not know how to begin naturally. Do not portray him as depressed, rejected, socially panicked, humiliated, or unsuccessful.

Keep his face, haircut, body proportions, and wardrobe consistent:

- Gray crew-neck T-shirt.
- Unbuttoned dark blue-gray casual overshirt.
- Black straight-leg trousers.
- White sneakers.
- Simple black commuting backpack.
- White wireless earbuds during the subway scene.
- Black smartphone.

In the home scene, remove the overshirt and backpack while retaining the same gray T-shirt, face, and hairstyle.

## Female Character

A Chinese woman in her late twenties with long straight dark hair, a natural side part, minimal makeup, and a warm, understated expression. She wears a loose beige T-shirt under a muted brown pinafore-style garment. Show her seated at a wooden table with an open laptop in a softly lit home interior.

## Product

Use one consistent 500 ml beer can labeled **BRAVE THE WORLD SUPER DRY** throughout the commercial.

The package has a blue top, a silver-white metallic body, bold blue SUPER DRY lettering, and a red label block reading **SUPER DRY DRAFT BEER**. Preserve the approved product reference's proportions, colors, and layout. All visible packaging text must be in English, correctly spelled, and consistent across shots.

Show fine condensation droplets, realistic aluminum reflections, and a clean silver-blue identity. Keep the can cylindrical and stable during handling. Avoid changing the label, inventing another product, distorting the typography, or turning the refrigerator into an entire wall of identical cans.

## Visual Style

Photorealistic contemporary Chinese city, premium youthful beer television commercial, cinematic 35 mm lens aesthetic, shallow depth of field, natural skin texture, realistic materials, soft highlight roll-off, and restrained contrast.

Use cool blue-gray tones for the subway and office. Introduce crisp silver-blue highlights in the convenience store. Use deep blue water and warm golden city reflections at the riverside. Transition to soft warm practical lighting in the home scenes. End with a polished deep-blue product composition.

Camera movement is stable, smooth, and restrained: slow push-ins, gentle tracking, and controlled static close-ups. Use foreground commuters, shelves, cans, and desk objects to create depth. Avoid exaggerated acting, influencer aesthetics, melodramatic staging, oversaturated colors, cyberpunk, and science-fiction effects.

## Audio

Use the same English-speaking male narrator throughout, sounding 25–30, with a clean, natural, slightly low-pitched voice. His delivery is relaxed, reflective, and quietly confident. Avoid announcer delivery or motivational-speech emphasis.

Narration is off-screen inner reflection. The protagonist's lips remain naturally at rest during voiceover. Speak only the specified narration lines. Do not add dialogue or subtitles.

Use a continuous understated electronic music theme. Begin with spacious ambient textures, introduce a gentle pulse in the office, add fresh electronic detail in the store, and resolve warmly through the riverside and home scenes. Keep narration clearly audible above music and environmental sound.
